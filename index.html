Field note · Matched-market test

Can a billboard prove it moved the needle?

The problem

Offline channels can't be A/B tested at the user level — there's no impression to randomize and no holdout to carve. A billboard goes up in Chicago, applications rise, and everyone claims the win. But how much of that rise was the billboard?

The approach

Find Chicago a statistical twin — a city matched on income, customer mix, traffic, business density, and seasonality. Detroit qualifies. Chicago gets the billboards; Detroit doesn't. Then compare each city to itself, before and after: a difference-in-differences design.

The call

Chicago rose by 300 applications — but Detroit rose by 70 with no billboard at all. That 70 is the market tide. Only the gap between them is the billboard's real effect.

The naive readout would have credited the billboard with +300 — overstating its impact by 30%. The twin city is what makes the number honest.

Field note · Portfolio funnel diagnosis

Applications were falling — was that actually the problem?

The signal

New-application volume (PIF) had been trending down quarter over quarter. Total portfolio volume looked fine on the surface — which made it tempting to file the PIF decline as noise and move on.

The diagnosis

An end-to-end funnel review told a different story: portfolio growth wasn't coming from a healthy new-customer pipeline — renewals were doing the work. Meanwhile, federal Do-Not-Contact suppression volume was climbing quarter over quarter, shrinking the population the PIF funnel could even target in the first place.

The shift

I brought the split to cross-functional stakeholders and reframed the question — not "why are applications down," but "how do we solve the suppression constraint that's shrinking the funnel." That moved the roadmap from optimizing PIF campaigns to fixing the actual input constraint.

Renewals were masking a structural funnel problem. Without separating the two growth engines, the team would have kept tuning PIF campaigns and never touched the constraint actually shrinking them.
